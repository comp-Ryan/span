## Ideas, ranked

### 1. Ledger on Call — text your accountant-agent (SAFE PICK)
- Problem: small-business owners can't ask their books questions. Finance work is slow and costly.
- What it does: owner texts "can I afford a hire in Q1?" or "anything weird last month?" over iMessage. Agent runs real-time analysis on the Intuit QuickBooks dataset (HF `CL251215/QuickBooks-Hackathon-2026`), replies with a sentence and a chart image.
- Model Learning angle: anomaly detection across the multi-company ledgers (duplicate vendor payments, off-trend spend, cash-runway forecast). Cross-company comparison is the twist nobody else will have.
- Track: Model Learning (or Agentverse). Challenges: Intuit, Fetch.ai, Photon, Gemini, Snowflake (load dataset), Sentry, Devin, Vultr, domain, Paper.
- Demo moment: judge texts the number, asks a question, chart comes back in 10 seconds.
- Risk: lowest. Dataset is offline. Only Photon is a live dependency, and SMS text is the most basic Photon path.
- Solo hours: ~16.

### 2. Relay — a phone agent for deaf and hard-of-hearing users (SWING PICK)
- Problem: phone calls are still mandatory for pharmacies, doctors, landlords, DMV. Deaf users rely on slow relay services.
- What it does: agent answers (or places) the call via Photon voice. Live transcript streams to the user over iMessage. User types; ElevenLabs speaks it to the caller in the user's chosen voice. Agent handles hold music, IVR menus, and "can you spell that" on its own.
- Track: Applied Intelligence (accessibility). Challenges: Photon (voice is their headline ask), ElevenLabs, Fetch.ai ("call the pharmacy for me" is pure intent-to-action), Gemini, Sentry, Devin, Vultr, domain, Paper.
- Demo moment: judge calls a number on stage, you reply from your phone by text, the judge hears a voice. Rooms go quiet for this.
- Risk: high. Depends on Photon 2.0 beta voice working. Decide in hour one: if an inbound call hits your webhook and you can stream audio back, go. If not, drop to idea 1.
- Solo hours: ~20 if voice works on the first try.

### 3. Thread — an agent that lives in your group chat
- Problem: group chats decide nothing. Trip planning dies in 200 messages.
- What it does: add the agent to a group iMessage/WhatsApp. It lurks, tracks open questions and decisions, nudges when a decision is stuck, books when consensus hits, splits the bill via Visa sandbox.
- Track: Applied Intelligence. Challenges: Photon ("agents that join human conversations" is literally in their prompt), Visa, Fetch.ai, Gemini, plus free stack.
- Risk: medium. Group-chat support in Photon beta is unconfirmed. Visa sandbox onboarding eats time.
- Why it's #3 not #1: demo needs 3 phones and the "decision tracking" is hard to make visibly impressive in 3 minutes.

### 4. Ramp — the new-hire agent you text
- Problem: first weeks at a job are 100 small questions nobody wants to ask.
- What it does: new hire texts "who approves my laptop?" or "what's the on-call rota?" Agent answers from a synthetic company knowledge base, schedules intro coffees, surfaces a 30/60/90 plan, escalates to a human buddy when unsure.
- Track: Applied Intelligence. Challenges: Workday (built for this), Fetch.ai, Photon, Gemini, plus free stack.
- Risk: low technically, high competitively. Expect many Workday entries; differentiation has to come from the channel (texting, not another web app) and from a learning loop (agent notices which questions recur and writes the doc).

### 5. Snap — photo-to-purchase
- Problem: you see a thing, you want the thing.
- What it does: text a photo over iMessage. StepFun step-5-preview identifies the product, agent finds it, Visa sandbox tokenized checkout, receipt back by text.
- Track: Applied Intelligence. Challenges: StepFun (runtime call required, this nails it), Visa, Photon, Fetch.ai, plus free stack.
- Risk: medium-high. Two sponsor onboarding paths (StepFun + Visa) plus product search needs a real catalog or a mocked one.

### 6. Check-In — a daily call to a parent or grandparent
- What it does: agent calls at 9 AM, has a two-minute chat, notices mood or memory anomalies over time, sends a one-line summary to family over WhatsApp.
- Track: Applied Intelligence (healthcare). Challenges: Photon voice, ElevenLabs, Gemini, Fetch.ai, Model Learning-ish anomaly story.
- Risk: same voice risk as Relay, weaker demo (hard to show "over time" in one weekend). Relay dominates it.

## Re-rank: UI-first (added after "I want something with a good UI")

Paper judges design fundamentals, ease of use, personal craft, human touch. A calm, single-purpose screen beats a dashboard. Each idea below is re-stated as a screen, not a chatbot.

### 1. Relay — the live call screen
The screen IS the product. Layout:
- Center: the call transcript, streaming in word by word. The other party's speech in one color; the agent's automatic handling (IVR menus, "please hold", spelling your name) shown dimmed so you can see it working without reading it.
- Bottom: a compose box plus three Gemini-suggested replies as chips ("Yes, that's fine", "Can you repeat the price?", "I'll call back"). One tap speaks it via ElevenLabs.
- Right rail: the call brief. Who you're calling, what you need ("refill Rx #4471, confirm pickup time"), each item ticked live as the agent hears it resolved.
- After hangup: a one-card summary (outcome, next step, date) with a "save to calendar" button.
- Phone leg: Photon voice. Web app is the UI; iMessage becomes a fallback channel, not the main surface.
Why it wins on design: it's an interface for a moment of stress, so every quiet choice (muted automation, big reply chips, no settings) reads as craft. Strong accessibility story for Applied Intelligence.
Risk unchanged: Photon voice must work in hour one.

### 2. Ledger — the morning brief
Not a dashboard. One scrolling page a founder reads with coffee:
- Top: one sentence from the agent ("You're fine through March. Two things to look at.").
- Three anomaly cards max, each with a tiny chart and a one-line "why we flagged it". Cards dismiss or expand; nothing else on the page.
- A what-if strip: drag "new hire start date" or "late invoice" sliders, runway line redraws live.
- A single question box at the bottom that runs the agent against the Intuit dataset.
Why it wins on design: restraint. Everyone else will ship a 12-widget dashboard. Lowest technical risk of the three.

### 3. Thread — the decision board
A live board next to a group chat: Open questions / Leaning / Decided columns, cards move as the chat progresses, a "book it" button appears when a card hits Decided. Beautiful concept, but the demo needs three phones and Photon group-chat support is unconfirmed. Keep as a stretch idea.

### Recommendation
Relay if Photon voice works at hour one. Ledger if it doesn't. Both are single-screen products where the design is the pitch. Build the screen first on Saturday with mocked data, wire the agent Saturday night, polish Sunday morning.

### UI build notes (either pick)
- Design the screen in Paper first (30 min), export to the Paper challenge submission.
- Stack: Next.js or Vite + React, Tailwind, one font pair, one accent color. Streaming via SSE from a FastAPI backend that also hosts the uAgent.
- Dark and light both, from the start. Judges demo on whatever laptop they're holding.
- Mobile width works, because the Photon leg means some judges will try it on their phone.
